# The Frost-Bound Colony: A Colony-Management TTRPG Master's Guide

## Section 1: Campaign Introduction and Philosophical Foundations

Welcome, Overseer Master (OM), to *The Frost-Bound Colony*. This book is an incredibly detailed, comprehensive, deep-dive adventure guide designed to assist you in running a systemic, colony-management TTRPG campaign of post-apocalyptic survival, resource distribution, and societal ethics. In this section, we will establish the foundations of our setting: its brutal, freezing environment, its systemic management mechanics, its core themes, and the grand three-act structure that will test the players' leadership to its absolute breaking point.

### Setting the Scene: Sector-4 Frost-land
The campaign is set on Sector-4, a planet frozen in a perpetual ice age. The sun is a pale, heatless white dot in a dull grey sky, and the mercury rarely rises above -40 degrees. The only hope for the remaining survivors of humanity is a massive, steam-powered Geothermal Generator built inside a deep volcanic crater. Around this towering iron monolith, the players must construct a colony, manage resource pipelines, and keep the flickering embers of civilization alive.
* **The Aesthetic of Survival:** Think of heavy brass pipes venting hot white steam, massive coal shovels scraping against iron, thick wool coats stained with grease and soot, frostbitten fingers clutching draft blueprints, and ice-covered glass domes glowing with a dim, amber electric light.
* **The Systemic Sandbox:** Unlike traditional TTRPGs where players are lone wanderers, this campaign puts the players in charge of a population of 200 colonists. Every decision they make—whether to ration food, enforce child labor, or cut heating to the residential dome—has immediate, systemic consequences on the colony's metrics.
* **The Emotional Weight:** Every colonist has a name, a family, and a role. If a miner freezes to death because the players diverted coal to the greenhouses, that miner's family will actively grieve, protest, or lose hope.

### Core Management Themes
* **Hope and Discontent:** Survival is as much a psychological challenge as a physical one. If the colonists lose Hope, they will refuse to work, steal resources, or abandon the colony. If Discontent grows too high, they will rebel, overthrow the players, and exile them into the freezing wastes.
* **The Moral Cost of Efficiency:** To survive a winter storm, players will be forced to make terrible compromises. Is it better to allow child labor in the coal mines to keep the heat on, or keep the children in school and risk the reactor shutting down? There are no clean, perfect answers.
* **Systemic Interdependence:** Every system is connected. Food requires water, water requires geothermal heat to melt ice, heat requires coal, coal requires miners, and miners require food and medical treatment. If one link in the chain breaks, the entire colony begins to collapse.

### Campaign Structure
* **Act I: The Cold Start.** The players take command of the half-finished colony during the first weeks of the freeze. They must secure a steady coal supply, build basic shelters, and deal with a food-rationing riot.
* **Act II: The Frozen Frontier.** The players construct a scout station and send exploration teams into the surrounding icy wastes, searching for old supply depots, recruiting wandering survivors, and establishing outpost lines.
* **Act III: The White Death.** The climax of the campaign. A massive, apocalyptic super-blizzard descends upon Sector-4, dropping temperatures to -120 degrees. The players must survive a seven-day siege of freezing cold, reactor failures, and societal hysteria.

## Section 2: Factions within the Crater

To successfully run this colony, you must understand the major social groups among the 200 colonists. These factions represent political blocks that the players must balance, placate, or suppress.

```
                 =============================================
                 |       THE CRATER'S SOCIAL SPECTRUM        |
                 =============================================
                                      / \
                                     /   \
                                    /     \
                                   /       \
                        [The Technocratic Guild] \
                        (Engineers / Efficiency) \
                               /               \
                              /                 \
                             /                   \
                            /                     \
       [The Laborers' Union] <-----------------> [The Keepers of the Hearth]
       (Workers / Human Rights)                  (Faithful / Religious Order)
```

### 1. The Technocratic Guild (The Engineers)
* **Overview:** The highly educated scientists and engineers who maintain the Geothermal Generator. They believe in absolute logic, scientific progress, and efficiency. They view the workers as raw labor units and advocate for automated control and strict rationing.
* **Key Figures:**
  * *Chief Engineer Charles Vance:* A cold, brilliant academic who speaks in statistics. He views the generator as his child and will sacrifice human lives to keep it running.
* **Methods:** Scientific blueprints, steam-automation, automated rationing, and control over the steam valves.
* **Attitude toward PCs:** Supportive, as long as the PCs prioritize the generator's maintenance and scientific research. If the PCs allow uneducated workers to run the boilers, the Guild will threaten to strike.

### 2. The Laborers' Union (The Workers)
* **Overview:** The miners, builders, and farmers who perform the back-breaking physical work of the colony. They represent the democratic heart of the colony, demanding safe working conditions, warm housing, and fair food distribution.
* **Key Figures:**
  * *Martha "The Shovel" Stone:* A fierce, charcoal-smeared coal miner who speaks for the common workers. She is protective of her union members and hates the Engineers' elitism.
* **Methods:** Labor strikes, protest rallies, resource hoarding, and sabotage of corporate machinery.
* **Attitude toward PCs:** Suspicious. They see the players as potential dictators. If the players provide heated housing and fair rations, the Union will work double shifts during winter storms.

### 3. The Keepers of the Hearth (The Faithful)
* **Overview:** A religious cult that has arisen around the Geothermal Generator, which they worship as a divine vessel of the "Primal Flame." They believe that the ice age is a holy purification process and that only prayer and spiritual devotion can save their souls.
* **Key Figures:**
  * *The Arch-Priest, Father Thomas:* A charismatic, blind elder who preaches before the glowing heat-exhaust of the reactor. He advocates for public prayer, spiritual discipline, and the punishment of heretics.
* **Methods:** Public prayer circles, spiritual healing, sacred ceremonies, and religious laws.
* **Attitude toward PCs:** Unpredictable. If the players support their temple and integrate their faith into the colony's laws, the Faithful can boost Hope to incredible levels. If the players desecrate the generator, the cult will declare them heretics.

## Section 3: Colony Management Mechanics and Dashboards

To run *The Frost-Bound Colony* effectively, the Overseer Master must track several key metrics at the start of each in-game Day. Use the following dashboard template:

```
                      =========================================
                      |       CRATER-4 COLONY STATUS          |
                      =========================================
                      | Population: 200 (150 Healthy, 50 Sick)|
                      | Geothermal Heat Index: Tier 2 (Stable) |
                      | Coal Reserves: [||||||    ] 60 tons   |
                      | Food reserves: [|||||     ] 50 units  |
                      | Hope Metric:   [||||||||  ] 80%       |
                      | Discontent:    [|||       ] 30%       |
                      =========================================
```

### Metric Rules and Consequences

#### 1. Geothermal Heat Index
* **Tier 0 (Shutdown):** The reactor is dead. Temperatures throughout the crater drop to match the wilderness (-40). 10 sick colonists die every night, and Hope drops by 30% daily.
* **Tier 1 (Low):** Only the core tents are heated. Greenhouses cannot operate. 5 colonists fall sick daily.
* **Tier 2 (Standard):** All residential and industrial domes are heated. Standard operation.
* **Tier 3 (Overdrive):** The crater is warm and comfortable. Sick colonists heal twice as fast, but coal consumption is doubled, and the reactor suffers a cumulative 10% chance of a steam rupture every day.

#### 2. Food Reserves
* **Standard Rations:** Consumes 1 unit of food per 10 colonists daily. Healthy colony.
* **Soup Rations:** Consumes 0.5 units of food per 10 colonists. Food is stretched, but Discontent increases by 5% daily.
* **Starvation:** No food is distributed. 5 colonists die daily, Discontent increases by 20%, and Hope decreases by 20%.

#### 3. Hope and Discontent Limits
* **If Hope reaches 0%:** The colonists lose all will to live. Work efficiency drops to 20%, and within 3 days, a massive coup will occur as a desperate mob attempts to seize the remaining scout vessels to flee.
* **If Discontent reaches 100%:** A revolution breaks out. The players are arrested, put on a mock trial, and banished into the freezing wilderness without supplies.

## Section 4: Act I — The Cold Start

The campaign opens during the first week of the freeze. The players have just arrived at Crater-4 with 200 survivors, only to find that the previous advance team has abandoned their posts, leaving the coal-unloader jammed with solid ice.

```
                  ===================================
                  |     CRATER-4 BASE MAP           |
                  ===================================
                                   |
           -------------------------------------------------
           |                       |                       |
   [The Tech Greenhouses]  [The Geothermal Core]   [The Union Bunks]
   (Food Production Area)  (Reactor & Coal Hopper) (Residential Area)
           |                       |                       |
           -------------------------------------------------
                                   |
                        [The Ice-Choked Mine]
                        (Coal Source Outpost)
```

### The Hook: The Boiling Point
On the morning of Day 3, a massive steam pipe near the Union Bunks ruptures due to a build-up of frozen water. The heating to the bunkhouses shuts down, and thirty workers are forced into the freezing cold. Martha Stone leads a crowd of furious miners to the Geothermal Core, demanding that the players divert steam from the Technical Greenhouses to heat their homes, even if it means the food crops die.

#### Narration Block for the Core Confrontation:
> "The air near the Geothermal Core is deafening, filled with the high-pitched, screaming hiss of super-heated steam venting from a ruptured brass casing. Plumes of hot white steam rise into the pale grey sky, freezing instantly into a shower of ice needles that clatter against your coats. Standing before the iron valves is Martha Stone, her face blackened by coal dust and her eyes burning with fury, backed by fifty miners clutching shovels and iron bars. Behind her, the residential bunks are covered in a thick layer of white frost, their windows dark and cold. 'My people are freezing!' she screams over the roar of the steam. 'If you don't turn that wheel and send the steam back to our homes, we'll rip those greenhouse pipes out ourselves! We can't eat if we're dead of frost!'"

### Resolving the Conflict
To handle this crisis, the players must choose one of the following paths:

#### 1. The Engineer's Choice (Efficiency First)
The players side with Chief Engineer Charles Vance, refusing to divert steam from the greenhouses to preserve food production. This requires the players to use force (sending their 10 armed Constables to disperse the crowd) or make a high-stakes Charisma check.
* **Systemic Result:** Discontent increases by 20%, and 10 miners fall critically ill from the cold. However, the food reserves are preserved, preventing a famine in the coming week.

#### 2. The Laborer's Choice (Humanity First)
The players turn the wheel, heating the Union Bunks but allowing the technical greenhouses to freeze.
* **Systemic Result:** Hope increases by 15%, and the workers return to the mines with doubled efficiency. However, the greenhouses are destroyed, reducing food production to 0. The players must find an alternative food source within 5 days.

#### 3. The Technical Patch
If the players contain an Engineer or a highly skilled mechanic, they can attempt to perform a dangerous high-pressure patch on the ruptured pipe while it is still venting. This requires a difficult Intelligence (Engineering) check.
* **Systemic Result:** If successful, both the bunks and the greenhouses are heated. If they fail, the pipe explodes, killing 2 engineers and permanently disabling the reactor's Overdrive capability.

## Section 5: Act II — The Frozen Frontier (The Outland Sandbox)

Once the players stabilize the crater's internal systems, they construct a Scout Station. They must select 5 colonists to form a Scout Team and send them out into the surrounding wastes of Sector-4 to search for supplies and survivors.

### Outland Sandbox Navigation
The world outside the crater is represented by an open-world hex map. The scouts travel at a speed of 3 hexes per day. They carry 5 days of rations; if they run out of food, they must return to the crater or begin starving.

```
                      ===========================
                      |    SCOUT TEAM STATUS     |
                      ===========================
                      | Location: Hex 0914       |
                      | Health: Healthy (5/5)    |
                      | Rations: [||||    ] 4 days|
                      | Salvage: 20 tons of coal |
                      ===========================
```

### Key Act II Locations
1. **The Steel Graveyard (Hex 0512):** The wreckage of an old colonist transport train. A search of the steel cars reveals 30 tons of coal and a crate of intact surgical instruments (heals 10 sick colonists instantly).
2. **The Weather Observatory (Hex 1419):** A high-altitude station set on a mountain peak. If the scouts recover the weather data plates, they gain a 3-day warning before any incoming winter storms.
3. **The Cave of Whispers (Hex 2205):** A thermal cave where 15 wandering survivors are hiding. The survivors are malnourished and sick; bringing them back to the crater increases the colony's population but places a heavy burden on food and medical reserves.

#### Narration Block for the Outlands Wilderness:
> "Outside the crater walls, the world is a featureless canvas of blinding white and cold blue. The wind is a continuous, howling beast that sweeps across the vast snow-plains, erasing your footprints as soon as you step. In every direction, ancient pine trees are encased in thick, glass-like coats of solid ice, their branches frozen in twisted, painful shapes. Your scouts move in a single file line, bound together by a heavy hemp rope, their breath forming thick ice icicles on their wool face-masks. In the distance, the sun is a cold, distant spark that offers no warmth, only a mocking reminder of the world that was."

## Section 6: Act III — The Great Blizzard

The climax of the campaign. The observatory plates reveal that a massive, historic super-blizzard is approaching the crater. Temperatures will drop from -40 to -120 degrees over seven days. The players must prepare their colony, stockpile coal, and survive the storm.

### The Storm Schedule (The Seven Days of Ice)

```
===================================================================
| Day 1: -50 | Wind rises. Greenhouse crops die.                 |
| Day 2: -60 | Coal mines freeze. Work shifts must be cut to 4h.  |
| Day 3: -80 | The water pipes freeze. Sickness increases by 20%. |
| Day 4: -90 | Reactor core crack. Steam leakage in District B.    |
| Day 5: -100| Absolute dark. Hope drops by 10% daily.           |
| Day 6: -110| The Union Bunks collapse. Workers demand Core beds. |
| Day 7: -120| The Climax. The Geothermal Generator redlines.    |
===================================================================
```

### Day 4 Crisis: The Core Crack
On the fourth day of the storm, the temperature drops to -90. The pressure inside the Geothermal Generator reaches critical levels, and a massive crack forms in the primary boiler cylinder.
* **The Problem:** Steam is venting directly into the atmosphere instead of the heating pipes. If the crack is not repaired within 12 hours, the reactor will suffer a catastrophic explosion, destroying the colony.
* **The Sacrifice:** Repairing the crack requires an engineer to enter the high-pressure boiler tube while the reactor is still running at half-power. The intense heat and steam will inflict certain death on whoever enters, but will seal the leak.

#### Narration Block for the Boiler Sacrifice:
> "The engine room of the Geothermal Core is a nightmare of iron, fire, and screaming white noise. The primary boiler cylinder is glowing with a dull, sickly orange heat, and a jagged, three-foot crack has opened across its curved face, spraying a continuous jet of super-heated steam that cuts through steel pipes like paper. The heat in the room is suffocating, yet frost is still forming on the metal floorboards only ten feet away. Chief Engineer Charles Vance stands before you, his face pale and his hands shaking. 'The automated bypass failed,' he screams over the roar. 'Someone has to go inside the maintenance tube with the welding torch. They have ninety seconds to seal that crack before the pressure explodes... but they won't make it out alive. Who are we sending into the fire?'"

## Section 7: Major Non-Player Characters (NPCs)

To make your colony roleplaying scenes rich and engaging, here are the detailed dossiers of the primary NPCs, complete with voice guides, secrets, and authority stats.

### 1. Chief Engineer Charles Vance
* **Description:** A gaunt man in his late forties with a clean-shaven face and thin, silver-rimmed spectacles. He wears a heavy leather grease-apron over a pristine white shirt, and carries a brass slide-rule.
* **Voice and Mannerisms:** Speaks in a precise, dry, monotone voice. He never gestures or displays emotion, even when the colony is collapsing. He looks at people as if he is calculating their weight in coal.
* **The Secret:** Charles was responsible for the death of his former assistant during the construction of the generator. He locked the assistant in a steam chamber to prevent a pressure rupture from destroying the core, and he has hidden the incident in his personal logs.
* **Colony Authority Score:** 15. If the players attempt to exile or arrest Charles, they must make an Authority check against his score, or his loyal engineers will shut down the generator in protest.

### 2. Martha "The Shovel" Stone
* **Description:** A stocky, muscular woman in her thirties with short, cropped red hair and coal-dust smeared across her nose and cheeks. She wears a thick, oil-stained wool coat and carries a heavy iron shovel.
* **Voice and Mannerisms:** Speaks in a loud, booming, working-class accent. She laughs easily but has a hair-trigger temper. She stands with her boots wide apart and her arms crossed, refusing to back down from any authority figure.
* **The Secret:** Martha was once a thief in the old world. She stole food from a corporate warehouse to feed her younger brother, who ultimately froze to death during the migration. She is driven by a fierce, desperate need to protect the children of the colony.
* **Colony Authority Score:** 16. Martha holds the absolute loyalty of the 150 labor workers. If the players lose her support, they will face a general strike that shuts down the coal mines.

## Section 8: Narrative Choice and Campaign Resolution

The final day of the storm presents the players with a profound choice regarding the political and moral future of their colony.

### The Three Paths

```
                             ===========================
                             | THE CLIMATIC DECISION   |
                             ===========================
                                          |
         -------------------------------------------------------------------
         |                                |                                |
         v                                v                                v
===========================  ===========================  ===========================
|  PATH A: RECTORSHIP     |  |  PATH B: THE ORDER      |  |  PATH C: COOPERATIVE    |
|  - Extreme efficiency   |  |  - Holy Inquisition     |  |  - Democratic council   |
|  - Engineers rule colony|  |  - Cult of Flame rules  |  |  - Equal resource splits|
|  - Iron discipline      |  |  - Public sacrifices    |  |  - High freedom, low coal|
===========================  ===========================  ===========================
```

#### Path A: The Technocratic Rectorship (The Path of Order)
* **The Action:** The players side permanently with the Engineers, declaring martial law and turning the colony into an absolute military dictatorship focused on scientific survival.
* **The Consequences:** Hope is permanently locked at 40%, but Discontent can never trigger a revolution. Work efficiency is increased to 120%, and resource consumption is reduced.
* **The Epilogue:** The colony survives the blizzard, but at the cost of its humanity. New Hope becomes a sterile, iron prison-camp where children are treated as future labor units, the elderly are quietly euthanized to save food, and the Geothermal Generator is maintained with absolute, cold discipline.

#### Path B: The Holy Inquisition (The Path of Faith)
* **The Action:** The players hand over political authority to Father Thomas and the Hearth cult, transforming the colony into a theological state ruled by religious law.
* **The Consequences:** Hope is boosted to 100%, but all scientific research is outlawed. Any colonist who questions the faith is declared a "heretic" and publically sacrificed to the generator's flame to "appease the fire."
* **The Epilogue:** The colony survives, but they have regressed into a medieval state of holy fanaticism. The generator is treated as a living god, and the colonists spend their days performing sacred rituals in the steam, completely cut off from the scientific history of humanity.

#### Path C: The Democratic Cooperative (The Path of Freedom)
* **The Action:** The players establish a democratic council, giving Martha Stone and the Laborers' Union an equal vote in all resource decisions.
* **The Consequences:** Discontent is reduced to 0%, but Hope fluctuates wildly based on daily resource levels. Work shifts cannot exceed 8 hours, and resource extraction is slower.
* **The Epilogue:** The colony survives the storm, but they are poor, cold, and exhausted. However, they have preserved their freedom and their humanity. They establish a democratic town hall, celebrate holidays in the steam, and work together as equals, proving that humanity can survive the frost without losing its soul.

## Section 9: Overseer Master (OM) Advice and Troubleshooting

Running a systemic colony-management game can be challenging. Here is practical advice for dealing with common issues that may arise at your table.

### Handling Micro-Management
With 200 colonists and dozens of resource lines, it is easy for the game to devolve into a dry spreadsheet simulator. To prevent this:
* **Abstract the Numbers:** Never ask the players to track individual pounds of food or coal. Use *Units* and *Tons* (as shown in the status dashboard) to represent large blocks of resources.
* **Focus on the Faces:** Whenever a resource shortage occurs, translate the numbers into a human scene. Instead of saying "You are short 2 units of food," say: "You visit the nursery dome and find that the children's soup has been watered down to a thin, grey broth. The head nurse looks at you with hollow eyes."
* **The Day-Night Cycle:** Structure your play sessions around a strict Day-Night cycle. During the Day, focus on work shifts, construction, and outland exploration. During the Night, focus on social encounters, political debates in the tavern, and the resolution of daily crises.

### Managing Player Disagreement
In a colony-management game, players will often have fierce, emotional disagreements about moral choices (e.g., "We have to enforce child labor to save the crops!" vs. "No, that makes us monsters!"). This is not a problem; it is the entire purpose of the game!
* **Do Not Intervene:** Let the players debate. Their arguments are the core roleplaying of the campaign. Only intervene if the debate becomes personal or stalls the game.
* **The Vote:** If the players are stuck, have Martha Stone or Charles Vance call for a vote in the council hall, forcing the players to make a decision and live with the political fallout.
