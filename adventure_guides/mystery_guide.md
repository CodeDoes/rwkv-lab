# The Blackwood Murders: A Gaslight Occult Mystery TTRPG Master's Guide

## Section 1: Campaign Introduction and Philosophical Foundations

Welcome, Detective Master (DM), to *The Blackwood Murders*. This book is an incredibly detailed, comprehensive, deep-dive adventure guide designed to assist you in running a gaslight mystery campaign of high-society scandals, occult conspiracies, and detective deduction. In this section, we will establish the foundations of Blackwood City: its rain-slicked streets, its societal divisions, its core themes, and the grand mystery structure that your players will dismantle clue-by-clue over multiple sessions.

### Setting the Scene: Gaslight Blackwood
Blackwood is a city of sharp contrasts, choking under a permanent blanket of coal smog and soot. By night, the streets are illuminated by the flickering, pale green glare of gas lamps, which cast long, shivering shadows across the wet cobblestones. It is a city divided between the opulent, velvet-draped manors of High-Gate and the filthy, cholera-ridden tenements of the Drowned Docks.
* **The Atmosphere of Secrets:** In Blackwood, everyone is hiding something. High-class lords carry illegitimate debts, respected clergymen participate in illegal séances, and the common street-sweepers are paid informants for criminal syndicates.
* **The Visual Style:** Think of wet cobblestones reflecting gas lamps, velvet tailcoats and heavy wool cloaks, horse-drawn carriages clattering through the mist, tea leaves spinning in porcelain cups, and old leather-bound journals locked with tarnished brass latches.
* **The Occult Undercurrent:** There is a thin, fragile line between rational science and the supernatural in Blackwood. While the police rely on fingerprinting and blood-chemistry, secret occult societies are performing forbidden rituals in the sewer tunnels beneath the city.

### Core Mystery Themes
* **The Illusion of Respectability:** Crime in Blackwood is not born of mindless violence, but of desperate preservation. The wealthiest suspects will murder, lie, and bribe to prevent their secrets from being printed in the morning papers.
* **The Weight of Evidence:** This is not a hack-and-slash adventure. If the players burst into a lord's manor with weapons drawn, they will be arrested by the Constabulary. To win, they must collect physical evidence, build a bulletproof case, and present it before a magistrate.
* **The Three-Clue Rule:** For every conclusion you want the players to reach, you must provide at least three distinct clues. This ensures that even if they miss or misinterpret one clue, the investigation does not grind to a halt.

### Campaign Structure
* **Act I: The Lord's Last Séance.** The players are summoned to Harrington Manor to investigate the murder of Lord Harrington, who was found dead in a locked drawing-room immediately following a private occult séance.
* **Act II: The Streets of Smog.** The players expand their investigation throughout Blackwood City, chasing down suspects, conducting stakeouts at gambling dens, and deciphering coded journals.
* **Act III: The Verdict at Blackwood Manor.** The climax takes place at a grand dinner party where the players must publicly reveal the murderer, present their evidence, and prevent a horrific occult ritual from being completed in the manor's cellar.

## Section 2: Factions of Blackwood

To successfully run this mystery, you must understand the primary factions. In a detective game, factions are not just army groups; they are sources of information, keepers of secrets, and gatekeepers of access to restricted areas.

```

                 =============================================

                     (Law & Order / Technology)   \
                               /               \
                              /                 \
                             /                   \
                            /                     \
       [The Society of Seekers] <---------------> [The Crimson Dawn Cult]
       (Academic/Occult Explorers)                (Secret Aristocratic Occultists)
```

### 1. The Blackwood Constabulary
* **Overview:** The official municipal police force. They are professional, heavily bureaucratic, and overworked. While they are starting to adopt modern forensic science, they are deeply suspicious of "amateur detectives" and are prone to closing cases quickly to keep the peace.
* **Key Figures:**
  * *Inspector Thomas Abernathy:* A gruff, pipe-smoking veteran detective who believes in hard legwork, physical evidence, and the social hierarchy. He is protective of his jurisdiction but can be bought with a bottle of good scotch or a clean case resolution that makes the department look good.
  * *Officer Higgins:* Abernathy’s young, eager assistant who is easily impressed by the PCs' deductive skills.
* **Methods:** Street patrols, forensic chemistry, jail cells, and legal warrants.
* **Attitude toward PCs:** Indifferent or hostile initially. They will treat the PCs as nuisances until the PCs present evidence that solves a case the police are stuck on.

### 2. The Society of Seekers
* **Overview:** A private gentlemen's club and academic society dedicated to studying the paranormal, ancient history, and rare antiquities. They possess the largest occult library in the city but are highly elitist.
* **Key Figures:**
  * *Professor Arthur Pendelton:* A blind scholar who can translate ancient languages by touch. He is obsessed with the precursor history of Blackwood.
  * *Lady Beatrice Sterling:* A wealthy widow who funds the society's archaeological expeditions.
* **Methods:** Academic archives, rare book translation, ancient relic analysis, and social influence.
* **Attitude toward PCs:** Patronizing. They will assist the PCs with translations or research only if the PCs trade rare artifacts or promise to keep the society's name out of any public scandals.

### 3. The Cult of the Crimson Dawn
* **Overview:** A secret society composed of Blackwood's most powerful elites, including judges, politicians, and industrial barons. They use their wealth to purchase forbidden grimoires and seek to use ancient blood-rituals to secure their political dominance.
* **Key Figures:**
  * *The Sovereign, Lord Julian Vance:* The charismatic, wealthy politician who secretly coordinates the cult's meetings.
  * *Madame Zeroni:* A foreign occult medium who conducts the cult's blood-séances.
* **Methods:** Aristocratic dinner parties, poisonings, black-market antiquities, and secret codes.
* **Attitude toward PCs:** Hostile. They will attempt to discredit, frame, or assassinate the PCs if the investigation gets too close to their secret membership roll.

## Section 3: Act I — The Lord's Last Séance

The adventure begins at Harrington Manor, a crumbling gothic estate in High-Gate. The night is rainy and cold. Inside, the household is in a state of absolute panic.

### The Locked-Room Mystery
Lord Edwin Harrington was found dead in his drawing-room. The door was bolted from the inside, and the heavy velvet window drapes were latched shut. The only occupants of the room during the murder were the five participants of a private spiritualist séance, who claim that the lights went out, a cold wind blew through the room, and when the candles were relit, Harrington was slumped over the table, bleeding from his throat.

#### Narration Block for the Drawing Room Crime Scene:
> "The drawing-room is thick with the sweet, choking scent of sandalwood incense and expensive cigar smoke. In the center of the room is a round mahogany table, covered in a green felt cloth. Playing cards, a brass pendulum, and a shattered glass crystal ball are scattered across the table. Slumped forward in a high-backed velvet armchair is Lord Harrington. His head is thrown back, his eyes staring wide at the ceiling with an expression of pure, frozen terror. A deep, jagged laceration cuts across his throat, but strangely, there is very little blood on his collar... and the silver letter opener on the desk, coated in fresh blood, lies five feet away from his reach."

### Suspects and Alibis (The Séance Participants)
To run a great mystery, the GM must have the details of the suspects ready. Below are the five individuals who were present in the locked room:

#### 1. Lady Evelyn Harrington (The Widow)
* **The Secret:** She is secretly broke. Her late husband had gambled away the family fortune, and she was planning to divorce him. Her alibi is that she was holding Madame Zeroni's hand on her right during the entire séance.
* **The Clue:** A search of her purse reveals a bottle of *Ladanum* (a strong sedative) and a secret debt ledger from a local docks-broker.

#### 2. Madame Zeroni (The Medium)
* **The Secret:** She is a fraud. She uses hidden copper wires, magnets, and phosphorus powder to fake spiritual manifestations. However, she did see someone move in the dark right before Harrington was cut.
* **The Clue:** Her velvet cloak has a secret pocket containing a silver-handled scalpel that matches the dimensions of Harrington's throat wound.

#### 3. Charles Harrington (The Nephew)
* **The Secret:** He is a drug addict who owes thousands of pounds to the Crimson Dawn Cult. He stands to inherit the estate if his uncle dies without an heir.
* **The Clue:** His hands are shaking during the interview. If the PCs inspect his jacket, they find traces of coal dust and a blood-stained linen handkerchief.

#### 4. Judge William Vance (The Friend)
* **The Secret:** He is a high-ranking member of the Crimson Dawn. He was having a secret affair with Lady Harrington.
* **The Clue:** He has a fresh scratch on his right cheek. He claims he got it from his rose bushes, but the blood under his fingernails matches Harrington's rare blood type.

#### 5. Sarah (The Maid)
* **The Secret:** She was secretly Lord Harrington's illegitimate daughter. She was standing by the door to manage the lights.
* **The Clue:** She claims she saw a "shadowy figure with glowing eyes" rise from the center of the table. In reality, she was bribed by Judge Vance to blow out the gas lamps early.


### The Time Limit Mechanic
To maintain tension, the investigation has a hard time limit of five days. On the sixth day, the Constabulary will close the case and arrest Charles Harrington (the easy scapegoat).
* **Investigation Actions:** Each lead followed, suspect interviewed, or crime scene searched consumes 4 hours (half a day). The PCs must choose their targets wisely.
* **Night Investigation:** Operating at night increases the risk of being ambushed by Crimson Dawn cultists but allows players to conduct stakeouts or sneak into offices when they are closed.

### Key Act II Locations
1. **The Blackwood Scribe Offices (High-Gate):** The local newspaper archives. A search of the back issues reveals a series of articles detailing a secret land purchase beneath Harrington Manor by the Crimson Dawn Cult.
2. **The Blue Dragon Gambling Den (Drowned Docks):** A rough, underground tavern where Charles Harrington gamblers. The tavern keeper, a scarred brute named "Two-Fingers Jack," will reveal Charles's debts if the PCs win a game of dice or intimidate him.
3. **The Apothecary of Dr. Bell (Smog Gutter):** A small shop filled with jars of dried herbs and chemicals. Dr. Bell can analyze any poisons, blood samples, or residues the PCs find, providing scientific evidence for the court.

#### Narration Block for Act II Investigation:
> "The rain in Blackwood never truly stops; it merely thins to a cold, greasy mist that coats your coat in a layer of gray soot. You stand in the shadow of a brick warehouse at the Drowned Docks, watching the entrance of the Blue Dragon. From inside, the muffled sound of low laughter, clinking glasses, and the rattle of dice filters through the damp air. Across the street, a lone street-lamp flickers, its gas-mantle buzzing like an angry hornet. The cobblestones are slick with coal tar, and every person who passes you pulls their collar tight and avoids your eyes. You have forty-eight hours before the police close the gates on this case, and the trail is growing cold."

### Act II Clue Progression Matrix
To keep the mystery structured and fair, use this matrix to track how clues lead the PCs from one discovery to the next:

| Starting Clue | Action Required | Resulting Discovery | Faction Link |
|---------------|-----------------|---------------------|--------------|
| **The Silver Letter Opener** | Take to Dr. Bell for chemical analysis. | The blood on the opener is animal blood (pork), meaning it was a plant to mislead the investigators. | Constabulary |
| **The Blackwood Scribe Article** | Search the archives for "Harrington." | Lord Harrington was planning to expose Judge Vance's cult activities to the newspapers. | Crimson Dawn |
| **Charles's Debts** | Question Two-Fingers Jack at the Blue Dragon. | Charles was promised his debts would be cleared if he stole Lord Harrington's precursor key. | Society of Seekers |
| **The Occult Cellar Key** | Translate the ancient symbols at the Seekers' library. | The key opens a secret vault beneath Harrington Manor used by the precursor cults. | Crimson Dawn |

### The Confrontation (The Grand Dinner Party)
The PCs must use their invitation (obtained through roleplay in Act II) to gain access to the manor. They must mingle with the high-society guests, collect the final pieces of evidence, and then publicly accuse the murderer before the guests and Inspector Abernathy.

#### The Accusation Mechanics
During the dinner, the PCs can stand up and present their case. They must lay out:
1. **The Motive:** Why was Lord Harrington killed?
2. **The Opportunity:** How did the killer bypass the locked door?
3. **The Weapon:** What was the true murder weapon?
4. **The Evidence:** Present the physical proof (e.g., the scalpel, the cipher ledger, or the blood analysis report).

* **The Crowd's Response:** Based on the quality of the evidence, the guests will either support the accusation or mock the PCs. If the PCs make a false accusation without evidence, they will be thrown out of the manor and arrested for slander.

### Level 3: The Crimson Cellar
If the PCs accuse Judge Vance or Madame Zeroni, the killer will panic and flee down into the secret cellar tunnels beneath the manor, where the Crimson Dawn is preparing to complete their final ritual.

#### Detailed Room Descriptions:
* **Room 301: The Hall of Tapestries:** A long, damp corridor lined with ancient, rotting tapestries depicting the precursor city of Blackwood. Behind one tapestry is a hidden stone door that leads to the ritual chamber.
* **Room 302: The Vault of Grimoires:** A secure library containing illegal books of blood magic. Guarding the vault are two *Cultist Enforcers* armed with heavy iron clubs.
* **Room 303: The Blood Altar:** A massive circular chamber with a stone altar in the center. The room is lit by bowls of burning green grease, and the walls are stained with dried blood.

#### Narration Block for the Crimson Ceremony:
> "The air in the cellar is cold, smelling of damp earth, copper, and rotting stone. You step into a massive, vaulted chamber where the brick walls have been replaced by rough, black basalt blocks that hum with a faint, sickening resonance. In the center of the room, standing before a circular pool of thick, black liquid, is Judge William Vance. He is dressed in the crimson robes of the Sovereign, a silver chalice held high in his hands. Slumped on the altar before him is Sarah, her hands bound in cold-iron cuffs. The pool of black liquid is starting to bubble, and a dark, slimy shadow is rising from the depths, its shape shifting into a mass of twitching, black tentacles."

## Section 6: Major Non-Player Characters (NPCs)

To make your roleplaying scenes rich and engaging, here are the detailed dossiers of the primary NPCs, complete with voice guides, secrets, and tactical stats.

### 1. Inspector Thomas Abernathy
* **Description:** A stocky man in his fifties with a heavy gray mustache and a permanent scowl. He wears a wool bowler hat, a tweed vest, and a heavy brown trench coat.
* **Voice and Mannerisms:** Speaks in a deep, gravelly London accent. He smokes a cherry-wood pipe constantly, tapping the ash onto the floorboards when he is impatient. He has a habit of jingling the loose copper coins in his pockets.
* **The Secret:** Abernathy knows that Judge Vance is corrupt, but he is hiding it because Vance is paying for his sister's medical treatment in a high-class sanatorium. He is torn between his duty as a policeman and his loyalty to his family.
* **Mental Defense (Deduction DC):** 16. To convince Abernathy to betray Vance, the PCs must present undeniable evidence of the Judge's involvement in the murder.

### 2. Madame Zeroni
* **Description:** A woman of indeterminate age with dark, exotic features and long black hair set with silver combs. She wears multiple layers of purple silk shawls and heavy gold jewelry.
* **Voice and Mannerisms:** Speaks in a slow, hypnotic whisper with a thick eastern European accent. She looks directly into people's eyes without blinking and plays with her tarot cards during conversations.
* **The Secret:** Zeroni is not actually a medium; she is a former stage magician who was hired by the Crimson Dawn to put on séances to distract the police. However, she accidentally summoned a real precursor entity during the last séance and is now terrified for her life.
* **Mental Defense (Deduction DC):** 14. If the PCs expose her stage tricks, she will break down, cry, and reveal everything she knows about Judge Vance's plans.

## Section 7: Narrative Choice and Campaign Resolution

The climax of *The Blackwood Murders* presents the players with a profound choice. Once they defeat the cult and stop the ritual, they must decide what to do with the *Grimoire of the Crimson Dawn* and the precursor gate beneath the manor.

### The Three Paths

```
                             ===========================
                             | THE CLIMATIC DECISION   |
                             ===========================
                                          |
         -------------------------------------------------------------------
         |                                |                                |
         v                                v                                v
===========================  ===========================  ===========================
|  PATH A: SYSTEMIC JUSTICE|  |  PATH B: EXPEDIENT COVER|  |  PATH C: SECURING REIGN |
|  - Expose the entire cult|  |  - Burn the Grimoire    |  |  - Use Grimoire to rule |
|  - Social chaos in city  |  |  - Police frame Charles |  |  - New Sovereign rise   |
|  - Constabulary reform   |  |  - City stays quiet     |  |  - Occult Empire forms  |
===========================  ===========================  ===========================
```

#### Path A: The Systemic Justice
* **The Action:** The players hand over the Grimoire and the cult's membership list to Inspector Abernathy, who uses it to arrest dozens of high-society nobles, including judges, councilors, and barons.
* **The Consequences:** The city's government is thrown into absolute chaos. The stock market collapses, several major banks close, and the streets are filled with riots as the working class celebrates the downfall of the corrupt elite.
* **The Epilogue:** Blackwood enters a turbulent, painful era of political reform. The Constabulary is cleaned of corruption, and the common people gain a real voice in the city's government. The occult threat is pushed back into the shadows, but the city is permanently scarred by the scandal.

#### Path B: The Expedient Cover-up
* **The Action:** The players burn the Grimoire, seal the cellar, and allow the police to blame Charles Harrington for the murder to prevent a city-wide panic.
* **The Consequences:** The Crimson Dawn Cult survives, though they are forced to go deep underground. The high-class society remains intact, and the newspaper prints a clean story of a greedy nephew who murdered his uncle for money.
* **The Epilogue:** Blackwood remains a stable, wealthy metropolis, but the corruption continues to rot its foundations. Charles Harrington is hanged for a crime he only partially committed, and the PCs are quietly paid a large sum of hush-money by the surviving cult members to keep silent.

#### Path C: The Occult Reign (The New Sovereign)
* **The Action:** The players keep the Grimoire for themselves, using its blood-magic rituals to claim leadership of the Crimson Dawn Cult.
* **The Consequences:** The players become the new secret rulers of Blackwood's occult underbelly. They use the precursor gate to summon entities that grant them wealth, political influence, and dark power.
* **The Epilogue:** The old elite are quietly replaced by the PCs' own loyal agents. Blackwood becomes a dark, gaslit empire of shadow magic, where the screams of the working class are silenced by summoned horrors in the fog. The players have solved the mystery, only to become the very monsters they were sent to hunt.

## Section 8: Encounter Design and Mechanics

### Running a Mystery: The Clue Ledger
As the Detective Master, you should maintain a "Clue Ledger" during your sessions. This is a private sheet where you track:
1. **The Clues Collected:** What evidence have the players successfully found?
2. **The Clues Missed:** What areas did they leave unsearched?
3. **The Hypotheses formed:** What is the players' current theory of the crime? Use this information to adjust the NPC responses. If the players are completely off-track, have an NPC drop a subtle hint or have Abernathy share a new piece of police telemetry.

### Mental Confrontation Rules
When interviewing suspects, use this structured system instead of simple Charisma checks:
* **The Suspect's Composure:** Every major suspect has a "Composure Score" (ranging from 10 to 20).
* **Breaking Composure:** To break a suspect, the players must present a piece of evidence that directly contradicts their story. This reduces their Composure by 2d6.
* **The Break Point:** Once a suspect's Composure reaches 0, they break down, panic, and confess to their secrets, providing a new major clue.

## Section 9: Campaign Timeline and Pacing Guide

To help you pace the adventure over multiple sessions, use this general timeline as a guide:

* **Sessions 1-3 (The Locked Room):** Introduce the crime scene at Harrington Manor. Interview the five participants of the séance. Search the drawing-room, conservatory, and servants' quarters. Collect the first three clues.
* **Sessions 4-8 (The City):** Expand the investigation into Blackwood. Visit the Scribe Offices, the Blue Dragon gambling den, and Dr. Bell's apothecary. Build the case against Judge Vance.
* **Sessions 9-12 (The Accusation):** Attend the grand dinner party at Vance Manor. Mingle with the guests, present the final case, and survive the subsequent chase down into the Crimson Cellar.
* **Sessions 13-14 (The Ceremony):** Defeat Vance at the Blood Altar, stop the ritual, and make the final decision regarding the Grimoire and the future of Blackwood.

## Section 10: Detective Master (DM) Advice and Troubleshooting

Running a detective campaign can be mentally demanding for both the GM and the players. Here is practical advice for dealing with common issues that may arise at your table.

### Dealing with Stuck Players
It is incredibly common for players in a mystery game to hit a wall and have no idea how to proceed. When this happens, do not let the game grind to a halt. Use one of these proactive techniques:
* **The Sudden Attack:** Have a group of cultist thugs or hired dock-workers ambush the PCs. On the body of one of the attackers, place a written order or a key card that points directly to a new location. This transitions the game from slow deduction back into action.
* **The Police Breakthrough:** Have Inspector Abernathy call the PCs into the station. He can share a new piece of forensic evidence that his officers found (e.g., "We found Lord Harrington's missing signet ring in Charles's room, but it's coated in a strange chemical. Do you know what it is?").
* **The Watson Technique:** If a PC has a high Intelligence stat, let them make a simple check. If they succeed, summarize the clues they have collected so far in a new, logical light: "Based on the fact that the window was latched from the inside and the maid was standing by the gas lines, you realize that the killer must have had a key to the service tunnels."

### Keeping the Mystery Fair
A mystery is only satisfying if the players feel like they had all the pieces of the puzzle before the final reveal.
* **No Secret Culprits:** The killer must be an NPC that the players met during Act I. Never introduce a completely new character in the final scene and declare them the murderer.
* **Don't Lie with Clues:** Physical evidence never lies. A blood stain, a fingerprint, or a written letter is always an objective fact. NPCs can lie, but the physical clues must remain the bedrock of the investigation.
* **Reward Clever Theories:** If your players come up with a theory that is actually more brilliant, logical, and satisfying than the one you wrote, do not be afraid to quietly change the culprit to match their theory. It makes the players feel like geniuses and leads to a fantastic campaign resolution.

This concludes the Gaslight Occult Mystery Guide. May your magnifying glasses stay sharp, and your cases end in absolute justice!
