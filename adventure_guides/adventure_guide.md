# The Sun-King's Tomb: A Pulp Adventure TTRPG Master's Guide

## Section 1: Campaign Introduction and Philosophical Foundations

Welcome, Adventure Master (AM), to *The Sun-King's Tomb*. This book is an incredibly detailed, comprehensive, deep-dive adventure guide designed to assist you in running a high-octane pulp adventure campaign of jungle expeditions, ancient traps, and global rivalries. In this section, we will establish the foundations of our setting: its golden-age pulp aesthetic, its cinematic pacing, its core themes, and the grand three-act structure that will send your players racing across the globe.

### Setting the Scene: The 1930s Golden Age
The campaign is set in the mid-1930s, an era of steamships, propeller planes, and vast, unmapped wilderness. It is an era where ancient mysteries still linger in the deep corners of the world, waiting for daring explorers to uncover them.
* **The Pulp Aesthetic:** Think of leather bomber jackets, dusty fedoras, roaring biplane engines, twin-engine flying boats landing on tropical rivers, ancient stone ruins swallowed by green vines, and golden artifacts that gleam in the beam of a flashlight.
* **Cinematic Pacing:** Action is fast, heroic, and larger-than-life. Players should swing from vines, slide under closing stone doors, and engage in fistfights on the roofs of moving trucks. Do not let rules-lawyering slow down the momentum of a great action scene.
* **The Thrill of Discovery:** The world is a treasure chest. Every forgotten temple, ancient map, and dusty museum archive holds a secret that can lead to fame, fortune, or global catastrophe if it falls into the wrong hands.

### Core Pulp Themes
* **Heroic Optimism:** No matter how grim the odds, there is always a way out. A hero can always find a loose brick, a hanging rope, or a clever wisecrack to turn the tide of battle.
* **Rivalry and Betrayal:** You are not the only explorer in the jungle. Mercenaries, black-market plunderers, and rival academic expeditions are always snapping at your heels, ready to steal your map or leave you in a snake pit.
* **Ancient Mechanics:** The precursor civilizations did not use magic; they used incredibly advanced, weight-activated stone mechanics, gear assemblies, and magnifying-lens lasers. Traps are logical puzzles that can be solved with observation and quick reflexes.

### Campaign Structure
* **Act I: The Museum Heist.** The players begin in a rain-swept metropolitan antiquities museum, defending an ancient golden tablet (the Map of the Sun-King) from an armed raid by the Obsidian Brotherhood.
* **Act II: The Jungle of Whispers.** The players fly to South America, navigating an open-world jungle sandbox on a steam-powered riverboat, dodging rival expeditions, and tracking the tablet's coordinate points.
* **Act III: The Solar Temple.** The climax takes place inside a colossal, mountain-carved precursor pyramid, where the players must navigate a gauntlet of ancient mechanical traps and confront the leader of the Obsidian Brotherhood before they can claim the golden idol.

## Section 2: Factions of the Lost Valley

To successfully run this pulp sandbox, you must understand the primary factions. In an adventure campaign, factions represent rivals, allies, and environmental forces that keep the players moving.

```
                 =============================================
                 |     THE RACE FOR THE SUN-KING'S TOMB      |
                 =============================================
                                      / \
                                     /   \
                                    /     \
                                   /       \
                     [The Explorer's League] \
                     (Academic / Heroic)     \
                               /               \
                              /                 \
                             /                   \
                            /                     \
       [The Obsidian Brotherhood] <-------------> [The Sun-Guardians]
       (Mercenary Plunderers)                    (Indigenous Protectors)
```

### 1. The Explorer's League
* **Overview:** A prestigious international organization of archaeologists, historians, and geographers dedicated to preservation and education. They believe that rare antiquities belong in public museums, not in private vaults.
* **Key Figures:**
  * *Dr. Henry Sterling:* A brilliant, pipe-smoking professor of antiquities who would rather spend his days in a library but is surprisingly handy with a whip.
  * *Evelyn Thorne:* A daring, wealth-seeking pilot who owns the *Whispering Wind*, a twin-engine flying boat.
* **Methods:** Academic research, museum grants, high-altitude mapping, and public lectures.
* **Attitude toward PCs:** Friendly and supportive. They will provide funding, flight transportation, and historical research in exchange for the PCs returning any salvaged artifacts to the museum.

### 2. The Obsidian Brotherhood
* **Overview:** A ruthless syndicate of tomb-raiders, black-market antiquities brokers, and militarized mercenaries. They view history as a gold mine and are willing to use dynamite, torture, and slave labor to extract precursor treasures.
* **Key Figures:**
  * *Colonel Ronald Carter:* A scarred, German-trained mercenary leader who wears a pristine khaki uniform and carries a gold-plated Luger. He has no interest in history, only in the highest bidder.
  * *Silas Vance:* A disgraced former archaeologist who was expelled from the Explorer's League for looting.
* **Methods:** Armed raids, dynamite excavations, bribery of local officials, and kidnapping.
* **Attitude toward PCs:** Hostile. They view the PCs as competition and will attempt to steal their maps, sabotage their vehicles, or ambush them in the jungle.

### 3. The Sun-Guardians
* **Overview:** An isolated indigenous tribe who have lived in the Lost Valley for generations. They are the descendants of the precursor builders and have sworn an oath to protect the Sun-King's tomb from "outer world" exploiters.
* **Key Figures:**
  * *Chieftain Xochi:* A stern, fierce warrior woman who fights with a Macuahuitl (obsidian-edged wooden sword).
  * *The Shaman, Tlaloc:* An old man who understands the ancient mechanical engines of the pyramid.
* **Methods:** Blowdart ambushes, jungle camouflage, animal calls, and deadfall traps.
* **Attitude toward PCs:** Hostile initially, as they view all outsiders as plunderers. If the PCs demonstrate honor, refuse to loot sacred burial grounds, and protect the valley from the Obsidian Brotherhood, the Guardians can become vital allies.

## Section 3: Act I — The Museum Heist

The campaign opens at the Metropolitan Museum of Antiquities. The night is rainy, and the museum is closed to the public. The PCs are meeting Dr. Henry Sterling to examine the newly recovered Sun-King Tablet.

```
                  ===================================
                  |    METROPOLITAN MUSEUM PLAN     |
                  ===================================
                                   |
           -------------------------------------------------
           |                       |                       |
   [The Exhibit Hall]      [The Scholar's Office]  [The Loading Docks]
   (Ancient Mummy Display) (The Golden Tablet)     (The Mercenary Truck)
           |                       |                       |
           -------------------------------------------------
                                   |
                        [The Roof Skylights]
                        (The Escape Route)
```

### The Hook: Smoke and Glass
While examining the tablet in Dr. Sterling's second-floor office, the sound of breaking glass echoes from the main exhibit hall. The museum's telephone lines have been cut, and the lights suddenly go out. A group of armed Obsidian Brotherhood mercenaries, led by Colonel Carter, have entered through the skylights to steal the tablet.

#### Narration Block for the Scholar's Office Raid:
> "The rain drums a steady, rhythmic beat against the tall arched windows of Dr. Sterling's study. On the mahogany desk, illuminated by the warm amber glow of a brass desk lamp, lies the Sun-King Tablet. It is a solid sheet of ancient, reddish-gold alloy, carved with intricate, interlocking geometric tracks and celestial maps. Suddenly, the lamp flickers and dies, plunging the room into shadow. From the great exhibit hall below, a sharp *CRASH* of safety glass shattering echoes through the corridors, followed by the dry, metallic *CLACK-CLACK* of machine guns being readied. A cold voice calls out through the gloom: 'Search the office, boys! Find the old man and bring me that gold!'"

### The Great Museum Escape
The PCs must protect Dr. Sterling and escape the museum with the tablet. Below are the three primary escape routes:

#### 1. The Skylight Swing
The PCs can climb up to the roof through the broken library skylights. This requires a Dexterity (Acrobatics) check. Once on the roof, they can use a heavy copper lightning rod and a coil of rope to swing down onto a passing coal truck in the alley below.

#### 2. The Fossil Hall Brawl
The PCs can fight their way down through the main hall, using the museum's exhibits as weapons. They can push a massive Tyrannosaurus Rex skeleton onto a group of mercenaries, throw ancient Greek pottery at attackers, or hide inside sarcophagi to launch surprise attacks.

#### 3. The Service Elevator
The PCs can carry the tablet down through the maintenance shafts to the loading docks. To do this, they must use a dumbwaiter pulley system or short-circuit the service elevator's electrical panel (requiring an Intelligence check). At the docks, they can steal the mercenaries' own transport truck.

## Section 4: Act II — The Jungle of Whispers

Once the players escape the museum and decrypt the coordinate lines on the tablet, they travel to South America. They board the *Congo Queen*, a rusty, steam-powered riverboat, to navigate the Amazon tributary that leads to the Lost Valley.

### Jungle Hex Navigation Mechanics
The wilderness of the Lost Valley is represented by a hex-grid map. Each hex is 10 miles of dense, trackless jungle. Traveling on the riverboat is fast but loud; traveling on foot is quiet but slow and exhausting.

#### Wilderness Hazard Mechanics
* **Piranha Rivers:** If the PCs must swim across a river hex, they must make a Strength (Athletics) check to fight the current. On a failure, they are swept downstream and take 1d8 piercing damage from carnivorous fish.
* **Malaria Swarms:** Humid swamp hexes are breeding grounds for disease. Spending the night in a swamp hex without burning citronella wood requires a Constitution saving throw. Failure inflicts "Jungle Fever," which imposes disadvantage on all physical checks.
* **Mercenary Ambush:** Keep track of Colonel Carter's progress. Every 3 days, roll a d20. On a 1-5, the Brotherhood has spotted the PCs' smoke trail and will launch a mortar strike or an ambush in the next hex.

### Key Act II Locations
1. **The Ruined Mission (Hex 0408):** An old 17th-century Spanish mission swallowed by vines. Inside, the PCs can find a hidden journal left by a dead explorer that details the water-traps inside the Sun-King's pyramid.
2. **The Canopy Village (Hex 1215):** A village of rope bridges and platforms built high in the giant kapok trees, inhabited by the Sun-Guardians. The PCs must negotiate with Chieftain Xochi to gain safe passage through the valley.
3. **The Gendarme Pass (Hex 2011):** A narrow canyon guarded by a fortified Obsidian Brotherhood outpost armed with machine guns. The PCs must sneak through the canyon at night or find a way to scale the sheer cliffs to bypass the blockade.

#### Narration Block for Entering the Lost Valley:
> "The steam engine of the *Congo Queen* coughs and sputters, sending a cloud of black coal smoke billowing into the dense, humid jungle canopy. On either side of the muddy brown river, giant palms and choking strangler vines crowd the banks, reaching down like skeletal fingers toward the water. The air is so thick with heat and moisture that it feels like breathing hot soup. Colorful macaws scream in the treetops, their brilliant blue and red feathers flashing against the emerald green foliage. In the distance, rising over the endless green horizon like a jagged grey tooth, is the Mist-Spire Mountain... the resting place of the Sun-King."

## Section 5: Act III — The Temple of the Sun-King

The campaign culminates in the Mist-Spire Mountain, inside the legendary Temple of the Sun-King. The pyramid is built of massive granite blocks that are perfectly fitted together, and its chambers are run by complex, weight-activated mechanics.

### Level 1: The Descent of Blades
The entry corridor is a long, low-ceilinged hallway of smooth basalt.
* **The Trap:** The floor is lined with copper pressure plates. Stepping on a plate releases a series of swinging pendulum scythes from the ceiling.
* **The Solution:** To safely cross, the players must study the carvings on the walls. The carvings depict the phases of the moon; only the steps marked with the "Full Moon" phase are safe to stand on.
* **The Action:** If the PCs fail, they must make a Dexterity saving throw to duck under the blades or slide across the floor.

#### Detailed Room Descriptions:
* **Room 101: The Hall of Whispering Statues:** A circular room lined with stone statues of precursor kings. The statues have hollow mouths that act as amplification tubes; speaking in this room echoes throughout the entire level, alerting any guards.
* **Room 102: The Dart Chamber:** A small room where the walls are pitted with tiny holes. Opening the chest in the center without disabling the pressure wire in the hinge fires a volley of poisoned darts.
* **Room 103: The Pit of Serpents:** A 15-foot-deep trench that blocks the corridor. The bottom is filled with hundreds of venomous fer-de-lance snakes. The PCs must rope-swing across or build a temporary bridge.

### Level 2: The Mirror Gallery
This level is a massive, dark hall containing five pivoting stone pillars set with highly polished silver mirrors.
* **The Puzzle:** To open the massive granite gate leading to Level 3, the PCs must direct a beam of sunlight from a ceiling shaft through the mirrors to hit a crystalline eye over the gate.
* **The Threat:** While the PCs are rotating the heavy stone pillars, Colonel Carter's mercenaries arrive. A firefight breaks out in the mirror gallery, where stray bullets can shatter the mirrors or trigger localized laser beams that cut through metal and stone.

#### Detailed Room Descriptions:
* **Room 201: The Lens Vault:** A small maintenance chamber containing spare quartz lenses and gear cranks. A search of the room reveals a *Magnifying Glass* that can be used to supercharge the mirror beam.
* **Room 202: The Shaft of the Sun:** A vertical chimney that leads to the mountain summit. If the PCs climb up here, they can open the primary solar shutters, but they must face a nest of giant mountain eagles.
* **Room 203: The Mechanism Chamber:** A room filled with giant, water-driven bronze gears that turn the mirror pillars. If the gears are jammed with a steel crowbar, the mirrors are locked in place.

### Level 3: The Solar Core
The final chamber is a vast, dome-like room supported by four golden columns. In the center, sitting on a high stone dais over a deep, empty pit, is the Golden Idol of the Sun-King—a life-sized golden statue with eyes of burning rubies.

#### Narration Block for the Solar Core:
> "The chamber is vast, dome-like, and silent. From a massive circular opening in the ceiling, a shaft of brilliant, golden afternoon sunlight pours down, illuminating the central dais as if it were a stage. Sitting on a high throne of white quartz is the Golden Idol. It is a figure of solid, gleaming gold, its face carved into an expression of serene, terrible majesty, and its eyes... two raw, fist-sized rubies that catch the sunlight and burn with a deep, crimson fire. Surrounding the dais is a wide, dark trench that smells of ancient dust and stagnant oil. Standing at the base of the dais, his clothes torn from the jungle trek and his face covered in sweat and grime, is Colonel Carter, surrounded by his three remaining mercenaries who are setting dynamite charges at the base of the golden columns."

## Section 6: Major Non-Player Characters (NPCs)

To make your roleplaying scenes rich and engaging, here are the detailed dossiers of the primary NPCs, complete with voice guides, secrets, and tactical stats.

### 1. Dr. Henry Sterling
* **Description:** A man in his early fifties with messy gray hair, wire-rimmed spectacles, and a rumpled linen suit. He carries a leather satchel filled with notebooks and a battered bullwhip.
* **Voice and Mannerisms:** Speaks in a rapid, excited academic tone, often pausing to adjust his glasses or scribble in his journal. He has a habit of clearing his throat before translating carvings.
* **The Secret:** Henry was once in love with Silas Vance's sister, who died during an expedition in Egypt twenty years ago. This tragedy is what drove Silas to the dark path of tomb-looting and destroyed their former friendship.
* **Combat Strategy:** Henry is not a soldier. He will hide behind cover, throwing rocks or screaming warnings to the PCs about incoming traps ("Duck! The tiles with the eagle carvings are weight-triggered!"). If pressed, he can use his whip to disarm opponents or pull down pillars.

### 2. Colonel Ronald Carter
* **Description:** A tall, muscular man in his late forties with a military bearing and a deep scar running from his left ear to his chin. He wears a pristine khaki uniform with riding boots and a leather gun belt.
* **Voice and Mannerisms:** Speaks in a cold, precise, German-accented baritone. He never yells; instead, his voice carries a quiet, menacing authority. He taps his leather riding crop against his boot when he is thinking.
* **The Secret:** Carter is secretly bankrupt. He has borrowed millions of dollars from European arms dealers to fund his mercenary army, and if he does not return with the Golden Idol, his own men will execute him.
* **Combat Strategy:** Carter is a master tactician. He will take high cover and fire his gold-plated Luger with deadly accuracy. He uses his mercenaries to suppress the PCs with machine-gun fire while he coordinates dynamite traps to collapse the ceiling.

## Section 7: Narrative Choice and Campaign Resolution

The climax of *The Sun-King's Tomb* presents the players with a classic pulp-adventure choice. Once they defeat Carter and secure the idol, the pyramid begins to collapse as the weight-sensors detect the idol has been removed.

### The Three Paths

```
                             ===========================
                             | THE CLIMATIC DECISION   |
                             ===========================
                                          |
         -------------------------------------------------------------------
         |                                |                                |
         v                                v                                v
===========================  ===========================  ===========================
|  PATH A: PRESERVATION   |  |  PATH B: THE RESTORATION|  |  PATH C: COLD HARD CASH |
|  - Return idol to museum|  |  - Leave idol with tribe|  |  - Sell idol to brokers |
|  - Global fame & funding|  |  - Temple sealed forever|  |  - Buy luxury yacht     |
|  - Brotherhood hunts you|  |  - Valley stays hidden  |  |  - League expels you    |
===========================  ===========================  ===========================
```

#### Path A: The Path of Preservation (The Explorer's League)
* **The Action:** The players run out of the collapsing temple carrying the idol and return it to the Metropolitan Museum of Antiquities.
* **The Consequences:** The idol is placed in a beautiful glass display case, where millions of people can view it. The PCs are celebrated as international heroes, receiving academic tenure, museum funding, and invitations to high-society galas.
* **The Epilogue:** The Lost Valley is opened to scientific research. A team of League archaeologists begins a systematic study of the precursor ruins, but the increased traffic attracts other black-market looters, forcing the Sun-Guardians to migrate deeper into the mountains.

#### Path B: The Path of Honor (The Sun-Guardians)
* **The Action:** The players decide that the temple is a sacred tomb and return the idol to Chieftain Xochi, assisting the tribe in sealing the mountain pass forever.
* **The Consequences:** The PCs leave the valley empty-handed, but they gain the absolute respect of the Sun-Guardians, who gift them a pair of ancient precursor silver daggers.
* **The Epilogue:** The Sun-King's tomb remains safe and undisturbed. The Lost Valley fades back into legend, completely hidden from the outside world. The PCs return to the city broke but with clear consciences, ready for their next adventure with nothing but their stories and their silver daggers.

#### Path C: The Path of Fortune (The Black Market)
* **The Action:** The players double-cross both the League and the Guardians, smuggling the golden idol to Europe to sell it to a private antiquities collector.
* **The Consequences:** The PCs become incredibly wealthy, buying luxury yachts, mansions, and private biplanes. However, they are expelled from the Explorer's League and have a permanent bounty on their heads from both the law and the Obsidian Brotherhood.
* **The Epilogue:** The idol is locked in a dark, private vault, never to be seen by the public again. The PCs live a life of luxury but must constantly look over their shoulders, knowing that the next knock on their door could be Colonel Carter's surviving mercenaries or a detective from the Constabulary.

## Section 8: Encounter Design and Mechanics

### Pulp Action Chase Scenes
To run a classic pulp chase scene (such as escaping a collapsing temple or chasing a mercenary truck on horse), use this abstract, high-tension system:
1. **The Chase Meter:** The chase is tracked on a meter of 5 *Stages*. The PCs start at Stage 2; the pursuers start at Stage 0.
2. **The Turn Actions:** On their turn, each PC can choose one of the following actions:
   * *Ram / Swerve:* Make a Driving or Athletics check to push a pursuer off the road, reducing their Stage by 1.
   * *Shoot / Throw:* Attack the pursuer's tires, engine, or driver.
   * *Brace / Navigate:* Make a Survival or Perception check to find a shortcut, increasing the PCs' Stage by 1.
3. **The Goal:** If the PCs reach Stage 5, they successfully escape. If the pursuers reach the PCs' Stage, they successfully board the vehicle, initiating a close-quarters fistfight.

## Section 9: Campaign Timeline and Pacing Guide

To help you pace the adventure over multiple sessions, use this general timeline as a guide:

* **Sessions 1-3 (The Museum):** Examine the Sun-King Tablet. Defend the museum from the Obsidian Brotherhood raid. Escape across the roof or through the service tunnels. Refit your flying boat.
* **Sessions 4-8 (The River):** Fly to South America. Navigate the Amazon riverboat sandbox. Explore the Ruined Mission, negotiate with the Canopy Village, and bypass the Gendarme Pass blockades.
* **Sessions 9-12 (The Pyramid):** Scale the Mist-Spire Mountain. Descent through the three temple levels. Solve the Phase Moon puzzle and the Mirror Gallery puzzle while under fire.
* **Sessions 13-14 (The Climax):** Confront Colonel Carter at the Solar Core. Escape the collapsing pyramid and decide the final fate of the Golden Idol.

## Section 10: Adventure Master (AM) Advice and Troubleshooting

Running a pulp adventure campaign requires maintaining high energy, dealing with ridiculous player plans, and keeping the story moving. Here is practical advice for dealing with common issues that may arise at your table.

### Managing Player Creativity
Pulp heroes are famous for coming up with insane, desperate plans on the fly (e.g., "I want to shoot the dynamite in the mercenary's hand while swinging from the chandelier"). As the Adventure Master, your default answer should always be: **"Yes, but..."**
* **The Rule of Cool:** If a plan is heroic, creative, and highly entertaining, do not disallow it because of a minor rule. Instead, set a high difficulty class (DC) and let the player roll.
* **The Cost of Failure:** If the roll fails, do not just say "nothing happens." Failure in a pulp game should lead to more dramatic complications: "You miss the dynamite and shoot the rope holding the chandelier. You plunge down into the middle of the mercenary group, your weapon knocked out of your hand."

### Pacing and Momentum
If the players are spending too much time discussing options or arguing over plans, the pacing of the pulp adventure will suffer. When the game slows down, use "The Raymond Chandler Rule":
* **Bring in a Man with a Gun:** If the action drags, have a mercenary truck crash through the window, a venomous snake drop from the canopy, or a localized earthquake shake the ruins. This forces the players to stop planning and start acting.
* **Visual Combat Descriptions:** Instead of saying "You hit the mercenary for 5 damage," describe the cinematic action: "You duck under his wild swing, deliver a sharp left hook to his jaw, and send him crashing over the wooden balustrade into a display of ancient armor."

This concludes the Pulp Adventure Guide. May your whips never snap, your biplanes always fly, and your fortunes be legendary!
