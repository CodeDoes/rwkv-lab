# The Osiris Breach: A Sci-Fi TTRPG Campaign Master's Guide

## Section 1: Campaign Introduction and Philosophical Foundations

Welcome, Space Master (SM), to *The Osiris Breach*. This book is an incredibly detailed, comprehensive, deep-dive adventure guide designed to assist you in running an epic sci-fi campaign of corporate espionage, space exploration, and ancient alien mysteries. In this section, we will establish the foundations of the Osiris Sector: its unique technology, its sociopolitical climate, its core themes, and the grand narrative structure that your players will navigate over dozens of play sessions.

### Setting the Scene: The Osiris Sector
The Osiris Sector is a frontier system cut off from the core worlds. A century ago, the primary Quantum Collapse Gate—the only highway back to Earth—mysteriously shut down, leaving the local colonies stranded. Today, the sector is a pressure cooker of dwindling resources, deteriorating high technology, and escalating tensions between corporate oligarchies, miners, and scrap-scavengers.
* **Technology in Decay:** There is no magic here, only highly complex technology that the current generation can operate but no longer fully understands or replicates. Starships are patched with sheet metal, life support systems rattle with duct tape, and plasma weapons are treated as heirloom relics.
* **The Visual Style:** Think of stained metal corridors, flickering fluorescent tubes, cold blue holographic projections, massive industrial mining rigs silhouetted against boiling nebulae, and spacesuits scuffed by cosmic dust.
* **The Atmosphere of Isolation:** Space is silent, dark, and indifferent. Help is never coming from Earth. Survival depends on resourcefulness, mechanical ingenuity, and the alliances the players form.

### Core Themes
* **Technological Scarcity:** Every battery charge, oxygen tank, and engine part matters. Players must manage their starship's fuel and structural integrity as carefully as their personal hit points.
* **Corporate Hegemony vs. Individual Liberty:** The mega-corporations own the oxygen you breathe and the bunk you sleep in. To survive, people must decide whether to sell their freedom for security or fight for independence in the void.
* **The Alien Echo:** The sector is littered with "Precursor Ruins"—the remnants of a post-biological alien race that vanished millions of years ago, leaving behind gravity-manipulating relics that are highly prized, highly illegal, and extremely volatile.

### Campaign Structure
* **Act I: Ceres-9 Incident.** The players begin as freelance contractors on the crumbling mining outpost of Ceres-9, investigating the apparent suicide of a senior cybernetics engineer who discovered a secret precursor signal.
* **Act II: The Void-Weaver's Path.** The players acquire their own starship (the *Nomad*) and navigate an open-world space sandbox, jumping between stations, dodging pirate flotillas, and tracking the signal's source across an asteroid field.
* **Act III: Entering the Osiris Breach.** The climax takes place inside a colossal, hollowed-out precursor Dyson-sphere component orbiting a black hole, where the players must decide whether to activate the ancient gateway or destroy it to prevent a catastrophic collapse.

## Section 2: Factions of the Osiris Sector

To successfully run this sandbox, you must understand the major power players. Do not treat these factions as static quest-givers; they are active, reactive, and always pursuing their own goals. Use the descriptions below to guide how their agents interact with the PCs.

### 1. The Solarian Hegemony
* **Overview:** A totalitarian corporate alliance formed by the remnants of the old colony administration. They control the primary agricultural biodomes and the remaining high-tech manufacturing plants. They believe that survival in the sector requires absolute central planning, strict rationing, and corporate ownership of all resources.
* **Key Figures:**
  * *Director Angela Sterling:* A cold, calculating executive who runs the Hegemony like a clockwork machine. She believes that individual liberties are a luxury a stranded colony cannot afford.
  * *Commander Vance:* The ruthless head of the Hegemony Security Forces, who conducts station sweeps with armed shock-troopers.
* **Methods:** Drone surveillance, blockade fleets, debt slavery, and genetic-patent enforcement.
* **Attitude toward PCs:** The Hegemony views the PCs as potential assets or illegal agitators. They will attempt to lease their starship or arrest them for "unregistered salvage" if they carry precursor relics.

### 2. The United Belt Syndicate (UBS)
* **Overview:** A loose union of miners, pilots, and engineers who populate the asteroid belts and scrap-yards. They are the blue-collar backbone of the sector, and they are tired of dying in deep-space mines to enrich the Hegemony's board of directors.
* **Key Figures:**
  * *Caleb "the Wrench" Stone:* A charismatic, scarred mining boss who lost his arm to a hydraulic failure. He advocates for a total embargo on raw ore shipments to the Hegemony stations.
  * *Nari Chen:* A brilliant, hot-headed hacker who coordinates the sabotage of corporate supply lines.
* **Methods:** Guerrilla strikes, strike blockades, black-market trading, and structural sabotage.
* **Attitude toward PCs:** Friendly, provided the PCs do not work for the Hegemony. They will offer cheap repair parts, safe harbors, and mercenary work in exchange for assistance in their union war.

### 3. The Cult of the Void-Weavers
* **Overview:** A fanatical religious cult composed of spacers who have spent too much time in deep space without radiation shielding. They worship the precursor ruins and believe that the "Void" is a conscious, divine entity that wishes to absorb all physical matter.
* **Key Figures:**
  * *The Arch-weaver, Apostle Gideon:* A heavily augmented cyborg whose organic parts have been replaced with raw precursor materials. He speaks in cryptic, mathematical riddles.
  * *Sister Teresa:* A fanatical pilot who leads suicide ramming-strikes against corporate science vessels.
* **Methods:** Suicide bombings, telepathic transmissions, sabotage of gravity-generators, and kidnapping of corporate scientists.
* **Attitude toward PCs:** Hostile and unpredictable. They see the PCs' starship as a temple to be cleansed or a holy vessel to be sacrificed to the black hole.

## Section 3: Act I — The Ceres-9 Incident

The campaign begins on Ceres-9, an industrial mining colony carved into a massive carbonaceous asteroid. The air is recycled, smelling of grease and iron, and gravity is maintained at a shaky 0.4g by a spinning central ring.

### The Hook: The Hanging Engineer
The PCs are hired by Nari Chen to investigate the death of Dr. Marcus Gray, a senior cybernetics engineer for the Hegemony who was found dead in his private laboratory, hanging from a crane wire. The local security forces ruled it a suicide and sealed the lab, but Nari suspects foul play: Marcus was secretly passing her decrypted telemetry data from the planet's deep shafts.

#### Narration Block for Dr. Marcus Gray's Laboratory:
> "The sliding metal door of Dr. Gray's lab stands open, its hazard lock shorted out in a shower of yellow sparks. Inside, the laboratory is in total disarray. Screens flicker with cascading green error codes, and a cold hum vibrates from a central quantum processor that has been smashed with a heavy wrench. Hanging from a yellow industrial crane cable in the center of the room is Dr. Gray. His cybernetic eye still glows with a faint, dying amber light, projecting a distorted, shivering hologram onto the blood-splattered floorboards below. The hologram depicts a rotating, geometric sphere, accompanied by a repeating audio sequence: a series of metallic clicks, followed by a voice whispering, 'The breach is opening... the weavers are calling.'"

### Investigation Path
To solve Marcus's murder and discover the secret signal, the PCs must gather clues across the station:

#### 1. The Broken Core
An Intelligence (Technology) check allows a character to recover the shattered memory core of the quantum processor. The data is corrupted, but it reveals that Marcus's last transmission was sent to a secure terminal in the Smelting Yards.

#### 2. The Autopsy Report
If the PCs bribe the local coroner or hack into the medical database, they learn that Marcus was dead before he was hung. His cause of death was high-voltage neural frying, suggesting his brain-computer interface was forcefully overloaded by a military-grade intrusion deck.

#### 3. The Suspect in the Neon Gutter
In the neon-drenched slums of Ceres-9, the PCs find a low-life decker named "Solder-Head." He confesses that he was paid by a Hegemony security agent to crack Marcus's home security grid, but he fled when he realized the agent intended to murder the scientist. Solder-Head gives the PCs the security agent's ID card, which grants access to the restricted mining levels.

## Section 4: Act II — Starship Navigation (The Void-Weaver's Path)

Once the players solve the mystery of Ceres-9 and escape the Hegemony Security Forces, they inherit or steal the *Nomad*, a retrofitted cargo hauler. This act transitions into an open-world space exploration sandbox.

### Space Hex Navigation Mechanics
The Osiris Sector is represented by a hex map where each hex is 1 Astronomical Unit (AU). Traveling between hexes takes 24 hours of flight time and consumes 1 Unit of Fuel (Helium-3) and 1 Unit of Ship Supplies (Oxygen/Rations).

#### Starship Hazard System
* **Asteroid Belt Hexes:** Entering an asteroid field requires the pilot to make a Dexterity (Starship Piloting) check. Failure inflicts 2d10 damage to the ship's hull armor from micro-meteoroids.
* **Radiation Clouds:** Shimmering nebulae that scramble sensors. While in a radiation hex, starship communication is cut off, and characters cannot use active sensors to detect incoming vessels.
* **System Failures:** Roll on the Ship Failure Table every time the ship takes critical hull damage or goes 3 days without maintenance.

### Key Act II Locations
1. **The Scrap-Fields of Osiris-4 (Hex 0509):** A massive graveyard of old colony ships. It is a haven for pirates and scavengers, but it contains valuable parts needed to upgrade the *Nomad's* warp drive.
2. **The Hegemony Blockade Line (Hex 1112):** A picket line of three military cruisers enforcing an embargo on the rebel mining camps. The PCs must either sneak through, disguise their transponder, or bribe the commanding officer.
3. **The Weaver's Monolith (Hex 1822):** A colossal, black stone needle floating in empty space, covered in alien glyphs. If the PCs board it, they must face Cultist zealots who are trying to broadcast the signal to the entire sector.

#### Narration Block for Deep Space Travel:
> "The steady hum of the *Nomad's* fusion reactor is your constant companion in the cold, empty expanse of the Osiris Sector. Outside the reinforced viewport, the stars do not twinkle; they are cold, steady pinpricks of light in an absolute blackness that seems to press against the glass. Behind you, the warm orange crescent of Ceres-9 has shrunk to a tiny spark. Ahead lies the Whispering Nebulae, a massive cloud of glowing violet gas and ionized dust that screens the precursor ruins from view. Every creak of the deck plates, every rattle of the ventilation shaft makes you wonder if your oxygen seals will hold for another jump."

### Level 1: The Gateway Hangar
The PCs dock the *Nomad* in a vast, atmospheric bay designed for colossal alien starships. The metal underfoot is cold and self-repairing.
* **The Puzzle:** To access the lower levels, the PCs must activate the hangar's power grid by re-routing energy conduits. They must use their starship's power core to jump-start the system, leaving the *Nomad* temporarily defenseless and without power.
* **The Hazard:** Re-routing the power activates the hangar's automated maintenance drones, which view the PCs as foreign contaminants to be vaporized.

#### Detailed Room Descriptions:
* **Room 101: The Control Spire:** A high tower overlooking the docking bay. The walls are covered in shimmering black liquid metal that responds to touch. The PCs can use this terminal to view a holographic map of the entire mega-structure.
* **Room 102: The Precursor Vessel:** A ruined alien scout ship sits in the corner of the hangar. Inside, the PCs can find a *Gravity Pistol* (a weapon that can push or pull targets with localized gravitational fields).
* **Room 103: The Drone Bay:** The chamber where the maintenance drones are stored. If the PCs do not sneak through, the drone fabricators will begin producing 1 new drone every 3 rounds of combat.

### Level 2: The Gravity Shafts
To descend further, the PCs must travel down a series of vertical shafts where the gravity-generators are malfunctioning. Gravity shifts 90 degrees every 60 seconds.
* **The Mechanics:** During combat, roll a d6 at the start of each round. On a 1-2, gravity shifts to the left wall. On a 3-4, gravity shifts to the right wall. On a 5-6, gravity reverses entirely, sending everyone falling toward the ceiling.
* **The Threat:** The cultist zealots have already established a base camp in the residential sector. They have set up barricades and are prepared to defend the gateway with their lives.

#### Detailed Room Descriptions:
* **Room 201: The Communal Slabs:** A series of giant stone platforms suspended in mid-air by magnetic fields. Cultist snipers are positioned on the higher slabs, firing down at the PCs.
* **Room 202: The Hydro-Bay:** An automated greenhouse where alien plants are grown without soil. The plants are highly toxic, releasing hallucinogenic gas if disturbed.
* **Room 203: The Generator Room:** The engine that powers the gravity grid. If the PCs can disable it, they can stabilize gravity for the rest of their descent, but they will also disable the lights, plunging the level into total darkness.

### Level 3: The Gateway Core
The final chamber is a colossal spherical room where a swirling vortex of violet light orbits a central singularity—the Quantum Collapse Gate. On a suspended platform over the black hole, Apostle Gideon and Director Sterling are locked in a final, verbal debate.

#### Narration Block for the Gateway Core:
> "The metal bridge under your boots is barely a meter wide, suspended over a yawning, infinite abyss. In the center of the vast sphere, a microscopic black hole spins, surrounded by a roaring, incandescent ring of stolen starlight and violet energy. The sheer gravitational pull makes your hair stand on end, and your ears ring with a deep, subsonic drone that feels like it's vibrating the very bones of your skull. Standing on the central platform, surrounded by flickering terminal screens, is Director Angela Sterling, backed by three Hegemony heavy troopers. Opposite her is Apostle Gideon, his metallic limbs twitching as they interface directly with the precursor control panel. Both turn to you as you step onto the platform, their weapons drawn."

## Section 6: Major Non-Player Characters (NPCs)

To make your roleplaying scenes rich and engaging, here are the detailed dossiers of the primary NPCs, complete with voice guides, secrets, and tactical stats.

### 1. Director Angela Sterling
* **Description:** A woman in her late forties with sharp, aristocratic features and silver-streaked black hair. She wears a tailored black military coat with silver buttons.
* **Voice and Mannerisms:** Speaks in a precise, clipped, mid-Atlantic accent. She never hesitates or stumbles over her words. She stands perfectly straight, with her hands clasped behind her back.
* **The Secret:** Angela is secretly a clone of the original colony director who founded the Hegemony. She suffers from a genetic decay disease that is slowly destroying her nervous system, and she needs the precursor gateway to access Earth's medical archives to save her own life.
* **Combat Strategy:** Angela stays behind her heavy troopers. She uses an advanced tactical HUD to direct her troops, granting them advantage on attacks. If forced into melee, she draws a high-frequency monomolecular blade.

### 2. Apostle Gideon
* **Description:** A tall, skeletal figure who is more machine than man. His face is a polished brass skull set with three glowing green optical sensors. His limbs are long and multi-jointed, made of a dark, alien alloy.
* **Voice and Mannerisms:** Speaks in a synthetic, buzzing voice that sounds like a radio transmission from a great distance. He tilts his head at unnatural angles and moves with jerky, insect-like speed.
* **The Secret:** Gideon was once Marcus Gray's scientific partner. He was corrupted by the precursor signal ten years ago during an expedition to the scrap-fields, and he murdered Marcus because the scientist refused to help him activate the black hole.
* **Combat Strategy:** Gideon uses his cybernetic implants to control the mega-structure's gravity-generators. He can hover in mid-air and throw heavy metal objects at the PCs using localized gravity fields.

## Section 7: Narrative Choice and Campaign Resolution

The climax of *The Osiris Breach* presents the players with a profound moral dilemma. The future of the entire sector rests on their shoulders.

### The Three Paths

```
                             ===========================
                             | THE CLIMATIC DECISION   |
                             ===========================
                                          |
         -------------------------------------------------------------------
         |                                |                                |
         v                                v                                v
===========================  ===========================  ===========================
|  PATH A: WEAPONIZATION  |  |  PATH B: THE RESTORATION|  |  PATH C: SECURING VOID  |
|  - Give Gate to Hegemony|  |  - Open Gate to Earth   |  |  - Destroy the Gate     |
|  - Build singularity gun|  |  - Sector reunited      |  |  - Total isolation      |
|  - Enslave the Belt     |  |  - Corporate take-over  |  |  - Self-governed Sector |
===========================  ===========================  ===========================
```

#### Path A: The Weaponization (Sterling's Way)
* **The Action:** The players help Director Sterling secure the gate, allowing the Hegemony to harness the singularity's power to build a fleet of gravity-weaponized war vessels.
* **The Consequences:** The United Belt Syndicate is ruthlessly crushed. Hegemony starships armed with singularity guns enforce absolute corporate rule throughout the sector.
* **The Epilogue:** The sector enters an era of cold, sterile peace. Security is guaranteed, but all personal freedom is abolished. The miners are relegated to debt-slavery, and the precursor ruins are completely militarized by corporate science teams.

#### Path B: The Restoration (The Gateway Opened)
* **The Action:** The players activate the precursor gate, successfully reuniting the Osiris Sector with Earth.
* **The Consequences:** A massive armada of Earth fleets pours through the gate. However, Earth is no longer the home the colonists remembered; it is now ruled by even larger, more ruthless mega-corporations that instantly claim ownership of the entire sector.
* **The Epilogue:** The local factions are instantly marginalized as Earth corporations take control. The Hegemony is absorbed into a larger corporate conglomerate, the Belt miners find their union outlawed by Earth law, and the sector's local history is erased in the name of global integration.

#### Path C: The Destruction (Gideon's Way/The Void's Embrace)
* **The Action:** The players overload the gateway engine, collapsing the micro-black hole and destroying the mega-structure forever. This requires defeating both Sterling and Gideon, then initiating a reactor overload.
* **The Consequences:** The Osiris Sector is permanently cut off from Earth, and the remaining high-grade precursor ruins are vaporized in a sector-wide electromagnetic pulse.
* **The Epilogue:** The mega-corporations collapse as their high-tech power grids are fried. The sector's population is forced to band together in independent, democratic mining cooperatives. It is a hard, poor, and manual existence, but the colonists are finally free to govern themselves without the threat of corporate slavery or alien corruption.

## Section 8: Encounter Design and Mechanics

### Tactical Combat Encounters
Use these environmental templates to make combat encounters in space stations and derelict ships dynamic:

#### 1. Hull Breach Hazard
* **Visuals:** A cracked viewport panel hiss-screaming as air escapes into the void.
* **Mechanical Effect:** At the end of each round, every creature on the map must make a Strength saving throw (DC 13). On a failure, they are pulled 10 feet toward the breach. If a creature reaches the breach, they are sucked into deep space unless they are anchored by safety ropes or magnetic boots.
* **Tactical Use:** Players can shoot cracked panels to intentionally create breaches, clearing out groups of light enemies at high risk.

#### 2. Depressurization Zone
* **Visuals:** Red warning lights flashing, thick metal blast doors sealing shut.
* **Mechanical Effect:** Oxygen levels are depleted in 3 rounds. Characters must either put on their spacesuit helmets (taking an action) or begin suffocating, which inflicts 1d6 damage to their physical stats every round.
* **Tactical Use:** Enemies without spacesuits will immediately flee or surrender if the room is depressurized.

### Custom Starship Combat Rules
When starship combat occurs, use this simplified, high-tension system instead of complex grid calculations:
1. **The Range Bands:** Combat takes place in three range bands: *Long*, *Medium*, and *Close*.
2. **The Roles:** Each player takes on a role on the ship:
   * *The Pilot:* Maneuvers the ship to change range bands or dodge incoming missiles.
   * *The Gunner:* Fires the ship's weapons (Mining Lasers, Plasma Cannons, or Missiles).
   * *The Engineer:* Re-routes power to shields, repairs system failures, or boots up the warp drive.
   * *The Hacker:* Scrambles enemy targeting systems, locks their thrusters, or hacks their life support.

## Section 9: Campaign Timeline and Pacing Guide

To help you pace the adventure over multiple sessions, use this general timeline as a guide:

* **Sessions 1-3 (Ceres-9):** Investigate Dr. Gray's murder. Navigate the station's industrial zones and neon-lit slums. End with a dramatic heist to steal the *Nomad* from a secure Hegemony hangar.
* **Sessions 4-8 (The Open Sector):** Explore the space hex-grid. Scavenge derelict ships, dodge Hegemony patrols, and negotiate with the rebel miners at the scrap-fields. Collect the three components needed to decrypt the precursor gateway key.
* **Sessions 9-12 (The Breach Descent):** Dock at the precursor mega-structure. Fight through three distinct levels of shifting gravity, automated defenses, and cultist barricades.
* **Sessions 13-14 (The Singularity):** Confront Gideon and Sterling at the Gateway Core. Decide the fate of the Osiris Sector and run the customized epilogue based on the players' choice.

## Section 10: Space Master (SM) Advice and Troubleshooting

Running a sci-fi campaign requires balancing hard science, high stakes, and high-tech options. Here is practical advice for dealing with common issues that may arise at your table.

### Keeping Starship Upgrades Meaningful
A common issue in sci-fi campaigns is that players treat their starship simply as a mobile tavern. To make the *Nomad* feel like a true character in the story, ensure that every upgrade requires difficult choices:
* **The Weight-Power Trade-off:** Upgrading the ship's shields or weapons should require more power, which can only be obtained by stripping down cargo space or installing a louder, less stable fusion reactor.
* **Visualizing the Upgrades:** Whenever the players install a new part, describe its physical appearance: a massive, rusted plasma cannon bolted onto the hull, or a complex maze of copper tubes running through the galley to power the new shield generator.
* **The Ship's Quirks:** Give the starship personality. For example, the *Nomad's* life support might whistle whenever the ship jumps, or the main computer might have an outdated, overly polite voice module that apologizes before every missile hit.

### Managing High-Tech Tools
Players in sci-fi settings have access to incredibly powerful tools like scanners, hacking decks, and long-range communication. This can sometimes trivialize mystery elements. Here is how to keep the tension high:
* **Sensor interference:** Precursor structures, radioactive asteroid fields, and solar flares can scramble scanners, forcing players to rely on their own eyes and boots.
* **Encryption Walls:** High-security corporate databases cannot be hacked in a single turn. They require physical access keys, which can only be obtained by roleplaying with employees or sneaking into offices.
* **The Cost of Hacking:** Hacking a system is never completely silent. Every failed hack attempt should increase the station's security tier, alert guards, or lock down doors.
